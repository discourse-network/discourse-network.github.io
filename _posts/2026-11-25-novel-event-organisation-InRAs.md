 Iain Souttar and Kat Phillips from the University of Warwick share the outcomes of their DisCouRSE-funded project, which developed training materials to help Innovation Research Associates (InRAs) organise innovative events that encourage knowledge exchange, collaboration and interdisciplinary working.


## A New Type of dRTP

Innovation Research Associate (InRA) is a new and expanding type of digital Research Technical Professional (dRTP) role aimed at producing impactful mathematical sciences research. In contrast to traditional academic pathways, InRA roles centre on interdisciplinarity and cross-sector collaboration. As a result, the ability to communicate effectively with both mathematicians and non-mathematicians is essential.

The aim of this 7 month project was to provide InRAs with the necessary skills to organise novel and effective events that facilitate knowledge transfer and collaboration between mathematicians and non-mathematicians.

## Delivering the Training

The project delivered a three-part training series for 12 dRTPs, providing them with the space to explore innovative approaches to event delivery and to experiment with new ways of fostering collaboration. The participants represented a wide variety of roles from across the dRTP space, including InRAs, data librarians, Research Software Engineers (RSEs), BioTech researchers and group leaders – both in universities and in industry. Participants were mostly UK-based, but also joined from India, Nigeria, and France.

The training consisted of three activities:

**The Event Laboratory:** a workshop where we looked at the anatomy of academic community gatherings, exploring the effects of decisions made by organisers, and how this can drastically change the outcomes of an event. At the end of the workshop, participants had the tools to organise and run novel and unconventional events. The event addressed both practicalities and more philosophical considerations.

**A summer conference season** where participants had the opportunity to lend a curious and critical eye to the academic events they attended, thinking about them in the context of the online training sessions. This was recorded in the collective event consciousness, shared between all participants.

**Two day workshop in September**, held at Warwick, where participants had the chance to share their reflections as well as put some ideas into action through some experimentation.

Those who could not attend the in-person session contributed to the discussion via the collective event consciousness.

## Project Resources Now Available

The workshops gave participants the opportunity to reflect on their experiences of events, both as organisers and attendees, and to try out some unconventional approaches to event delivery. This was done, as described above, in three distinct parts which informed each other. The ideas and reflections explored in the training were captured in the training materials.

We hope these resources will be useful to others interested in rethinking academic and community events, and that the project will continue to empower participants to experiment with new approaches in their own communities. The notes could also provide inspiration for others interested in running similar sessions. We welcome anyone interested in developing similar training, or otherwise collaborating, to get in touch.

## Putting Ideas into Practice

One particular highlight for us was in being able to attend an event organised by one of the participants soon after the end of the training. The event was a great success, and it was wonderful to see some of the techniques discussed in the training implemented 'in the real world'. We hope the training will continue to empower the participants to be creative in their organisation of events.

Another highlight was bringing together participants from such varied backgrounds, including InRAs, data librarians, RSEs, researchers and group leaders, working across academia and industry. Having participants join from different nationalities and continents also brought a range of perspectives and experiences to the discussions, ensuring representative exploration.

The energy from the participants in both the online and in-person sessions was fantastic. An example of this was the exercise of talking about each person's favourite event, which produced some very evocative and thoughtful descriptions. This created a real collaborative feel, where there was support and encouragement throughout, in turn allowing innovative ideas to be developed during the discussions.

## Quote

**Iain Souttar, Innovation Research Associate (InRA), University of Warwick**

"This project allowed us to move towards addressing a gap in current training for dRTPs. The ability to design productive spaces, bringing together people with varied expertise, is particularly important in dRTP roles. We were able to host a pilot training initiative, named Unorgodox. We plan to move forward with this through a further DisCouRSE funding proposal. Having developed good relationships with the [Digital Research Academy](https://digital-research.academy/) and Heidi Seibold (DRA), who took the online sessions, as well as having the comprehensive shared notes from the training, will help to create a sustainable version of the training in the future."
